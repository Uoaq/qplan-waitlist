## Post 2: against the slop (image 04)

Most AI in planning is a confident guess.

A chat box over a model, a fluent answer, and no way to check where any of it came from. In planning, that is how an invented appeal decision ends up in front of a client.

QPlan is built the other way round:
- Figures come from the data by fixed rules. The model writes prose and never authors a number.
- The assistant cites from 65 pinned statutory provisions and 28 judgments. A case that is not on its register is named, never cited.
- When a record is missing, it says unknown. Never none.

Delete where inappropriate.

QPlan is not only for planners.

Anyone deciding what to do with land in England needs the same thing first: the evidence, before the money.

- Developers and housebuilders: screen before you offer, then feasibility and full viability.

- Planning consultants: answers from pinned statute, every citation checked.
- Architects: the constraints over a site before the first sketch.

England only, and an evidence tool, not legal advice. Waitlist at qplan.co.uk.

## Post 4: the story (image 06)

"Somebody's already done it."

That's what I was told in third year at Cardiff, when I said I wanted to build an app to speed up the planning process. Honestly, it demotivated me for a while. Then I thought, fine, somebody's done it, but why can't I try?

I made it my dissertation instead: AI in planning, and what could go wrong. Everyone I interviewed said the same thing in different ways. AI should help the planner, not replace them, and you have to check the data before you trust it.

The first QPlan was a chat window with no model behind it, because I didn't know how to use API keys. I've been chipping away at it ever since, and it now screens sites across England and never makes up a figure.

I've written the whole thing up as a planning history, because what else would it be: qplan.co.uk/story
